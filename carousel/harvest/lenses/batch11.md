### ad-938391302530187 — text-card — A1 — 4 slides — metric runDays=null, baseline advertiser median runDays=null
**⭐ THE EXCEPTION OF THIS BATCH. The only deck here that is designed rather than photographed, and the only one that ends on a CTA-and-price slide. Described at length below.**

Advertiser: **AYANA** (creative for *AYANA Heights Seaview Residence*, Phuket — see the category note at the end of this entry).

**Format & grid.** Square 1:1, 600×600, full-bleed, **no letterboxing on any slide**. Every slide is one photograph with type laid directly on it — no cards, no split layouts, no collage.

**The fixed lockup.** The wordmark `AYANA` sits **top-centre on all four slides**, in identical position, size and colour (white), set in a **thin/light all-caps sans with very wide letter-spacing** (roughly Futura/Avenir-adjacent geometric proportions, a sharp-apex A). This one repeated element is what makes the deck read as a set — the headline block itself moves from slide to slide.

**The type system — a two-weight pair, used identically on every slide.** Each headline mixes:
- a **heavy/semibold ALL-CAPS line** carrying the noun ("WHAT IF", "A VIEW OF / THE ANDAMAN SEA", "THE FLOOR", "THE CEILING", "THE COMFORT", "YOUR HOME"), and
- a **light-weight, physically smaller, sentence-case or italic connector** carrying the grammar ("Every Morning…", "with", "to", "FROM" in italic, "FROM"/"OF" in light).

The connector is always set at roughly half the cap-height of the emphasised line and sits inset from it, so the eye lands on the noun first and the sentence resolves second. It is a single humanist-geometric sans family throughout — no second family, no script, no serif.

**The contrast devices — one kit, four applications.** This is the part worth copying:
1. **Slide 1 — translucent brand ribbon.** The photo is a near-white bed (white sheets, cream sleeve, white plate) — the hardest possible ground for white type. The fix is a **soft sage-green organic swoosh**, a wide curved translucent band sweeping from the right edge through the upper third, sitting *behind* the headline only. It is not a rectangle and not a full-width scrim: it is a shaped brand element that happens to do a scrim's job. White type on it holds at feed size.
2. **Slide 2 — no device at all.** The photograph's own lower third (dark timber floor and shadowed sofa) is already dark, so the white headline sits directly on it, bottom-left. The designer used a scrim only where the photo failed to provide one.
3. **Slide 3 — a solid knock-out bar.** On a mid-tone blurred close-up of an eye, the first line `A SEAVIEW FROM` is reversed out of a **solid dark-sage rectangle**, tight to the text with small even padding, left-aligned and flush to the left margin. The two lines below it (`THE FLOOR`, `THE CEILING`) sit plain-white on the softest, most out-of-focus region of the image. So: bar for the small line, bare white for the big lines — exactly the inverse of the usual instinct, and it works because the big lines are large enough to survive without one.
4. **Slide 4 — the full kit at once.** Sage swoosh bottom-right, a **solid sage knock-out button** (`SIGN UP` in white caps, `GET EXCLUSIVE DEALS` in smaller light caps beneath), the price block bottom-left, and an **award badge lockup top-left** (black-and-gold vertical shield: "DOT PROPERTY SOUTHEAST ASIA AWARDS 2024 / WINNER / BEST NEW LAUNCH SEAVIEW RESIDENTIAL").

**How the number is presented (slide 4).** `15MB*` is set **enormous** — roughly a quarter of the slide height, the largest type in the whole batch — with `15` in heavy weight, `MB` slightly smaller but same weight, and a small superscript asterisk. Above it, in two small lines: `2 BEDROOM UNITS` (the `2` in light italic, the words in light caps) / `STARTING AT`. Classic price-lockup hierarchy: qualifier small, number huge.

**Palette.** Sage-green + white as the only brand colours, over warm-neutral photography (cream, gold, skin, sunset amber) on all four slides. Gold appears once, in the award badge. Nothing else.

**Slide-1 hook text, verbatim (on image):** `WHAT IF` / `Every Morning…`

**Slide 2:** `BEGAN` / `with A VIEW OF` / `THE ANDAMAN SEA` — i.e. **slide 2 is the grammatical completion of slide 1's sentence.** The copy is one continuous sentence broken across the deck ("WHAT IF every morning… BEGAN with a view of the Andaman Sea… a seaview from the floor to the ceiling… from the comfort of your home"). That is a real, mechanical reason to swipe — no other deck in this batch gives one.

**How it ends:** on slide 4, the CTA/price/award slide described above. **This directly contradicts wave 1's "almost none ends on a CTA/logo/price slide."**

**Alignment/consistency caveat, honestly stated:** the headline block is *not* on a fixed grid — slide 1 upper-right, slide 2 lower-left, slide 3 mid-left, slide 4 upper-left. Consistency is carried by the wordmark position, the two-weight type pair, the sage palette and the running sentence, not by a repeated coordinate. For a numbered Hebrew listicle the opposite choice (fixed coordinate) would probably be safer, but this deck shows a working alternative.

**On-slide emoji:** none — 🤫🌊 appear in the caption only. Confirms wave 1.

⚠️ **Category note:** this is a **residences / real-estate** ad ("2 BEDROOM UNITS STARTING AT 15MB", "Every owner receives AYANA's Circle and Islander+ Card privileges"), run under a hotel brand and harvested as hospitality. It is not selling hotel nights. Real-estate money is very likely *why* this is the one deck with a designed template — worth remembering before treating it as a hospitality-budget benchmark.

## Batch-level observations

### Wave-1 claims — did they hold here?

**1. "Almost every travel ad carries ZERO text on the creative; all copy lives in the caption." — HELD, and hard. 11 of 12 decks carry no on-image copy at all** (National Parks of Japan ×2, Costa del Cars, thesushijournal, Review Vietnam, Dreamcatcher Tipi, Millennium, Discover Puerto Rico, Luxury Deals 4 U, GolfNow; sensible.magazine has a masthead but no slide copy). **10 different advertisers.**
- **Contradicted by exactly one deck: `ad-938391302530187` (AYANA).** Every slide is a designed text card.
- The strongest version of this finding is not the count, it is **what the captions contain**. Four advertisers wrote structured, numbered or priced copy and put none of it on the artwork:
  - **thesushijournal** — a literal `1. / 2. / 3.` numbered listicle, entirely in the caption.
  - **Luxury Deals 4 U** — £5440 / £5180 / £175 deposit / 10 nights, entirely in the caption.
  - **Dreamcatcher Tipi Hotel** — a bulleted amenity spec list, entirely in the caption.
  - **Review Vietnam** — times and best-time-to-visit rules ("⏰ From 4:00 PM to 6…"), entirely in the caption.
  These are four advertisers who *already had* listicle-shaped content and chose not to render it. For the commissioner that is the competitive gap, stated as a fact rather than an opinion.

**2. "Almost none ends on a CTA/logo/price slide." — HELD for 11 of 12; contradicted by AYANA.**
- Ten advertisers end on an undifferentiated photograph, several on the *weakest* frame in the deck (Dreamcatcher ends on an underexposed interior; Costa del Cars, Review Vietnam, GolfNow and Luxury Deals 4 U all end on letterboxed filler).
- **AYANA ends on a full CTA slide**: sage knock-out `SIGN UP` button + `GET EXCLUSIVE DEALS` + a giant `15MB*` price lockup + an award badge. It is the only closing slide in the batch that asks for anything.
- Note the asymmetry: **Millennium, GolfNow and Discover Puerto Rico do have CTAs and offers — but delegated entirely to Meta's link-card chrome** ("Book Now", "Find tee times", "Learn More"). Three advertisers, three different verticals, same delegation. Their slides are therefore worthless the moment they are screenshotted or re-shared outside the ad unit.

**3. "Emoji appear in captions, not on artwork." — HELD, 12/12, zero exceptions.** Emoji-heavy captions across 7 advertisers (National Parks of Japan 🐾🦦🥰✨, Costa del Cars 🏰😀🇪🇸☀️💯, thesushijournal ✈️, Review Vietnam ✨⏰, Dreamcatcher 🌲✨🌟, AYANA 🤫🌊, Luxury Deals 4 U 😍❤️🇱🇨💫👀, GolfNow ⛳⛳️) and **not one emoji rendered onto a slide.** GolfNow extends the rule: ⛳️ appears even in the *link-card headline* but still never on the image.

**4. "Aspect-ratio letterboxing (grey bars) is common on the ad side." — HELD, and it is the dominant defect of this batch. 7 of 11 advertisers ship letterboxed slides:** Costa del Cars (slide 4), thesushijournal (slides 2–3, pillarbox), Review Vietnam (slide 4), Dreamcatcher Tipi (slides 1, 2, 4), Discover Puerto Rico (slides 2–3, pillarbox), Luxury Deals 4 U (6 of 7), GolfNow (4 of 4, two of them severe).
- **AYANA is the only multi-slide deck with zero letterboxing on any slide** — the same advertiser that is the only one with designed type. Designing the slide and sizing the slide are the same discipline, and in this batch they appear together exactly once.

### Other patterns

- **Slide 2 is never used as a turn.** In 11 of 12 decks slide 2 is "another photo of the same thing" — a second stoat, a second angle of the castle, a second bedroom, a second golf course, a second activity. **AYANA is the sole deck where slide 2 does work**: it completes slide 1's sentence grammatically ("WHAT IF Every Morning…" → "BEGAN with A VIEW OF THE ANDAMAN SEA"), which is the only mechanical swipe incentive in the batch. 1 advertiser out of 11.
- **Phone-snap creative in paid placements — 2 advertisers, both businesses:** Costa del Cars (tourists in-frame, blown highlights) and thesushijournal (an uncomposed close-up as the hook slide). These are ads, not organic posts, and they still look like organic posts.
- **Supplier-stock reuse — 2 advertisers:** Luxury Deals 4 U runs Sandals' own photography with the chain's watermark still visible (slides 4, 7); Millennium runs what reads as dated brochure stock. Neither adds a single identifying mark of their own.
- **Longest-running creative ≠ best-made creative.** The two decks with a real `runDays` number are **Dreamcatcher Tipi Hotel (283 days, still active)** and **Discover Puerto Rico (66 days, still active)** — both plain, untyped photo dumps, both letterboxed. AYANA, the only designed deck, ran 20 days (May 21 – Jun 9 2026) and is inactive. **With no advertiser-median baseline available for any deck in this batch, none of this supports a performance claim in either direction — it only rules out "designed decks obviously run longer" as something this batch can show.**
