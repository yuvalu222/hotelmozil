## Batch-level observations

Counts below are of **distinct accounts/advertisers within batch 5 only**. Eight distinct advertisers appear across the 8 A1 entries — Pacifico Living, flyeast, אחוזת הספא, בני ביטון, יוני פארג' אילת, Lear Sense (2 entries, one creative), Miri Hamias, patric_palm — and four distinct accounts across the 4 A2 entries: steveandnes, whatshappening365, mustvisitjapan, crete_travel_guide.

1. **Text-on-image is an organic habit, not an ad habit.** All 4 of 4 A2 accounts burn text into slide 1 (steveandnes, whatshappening365, mustvisitjapan, crete_travel_guide). Among the A1 ads, **7 of 8 advertisers put zero text on any slide** (Pacifico Living, אחוזת הספא, בני ביטון, יוני פארג', Lear Sense, Miri Hamias, patric_palm) and delegate 100% of the copy to the platform body text and CTA button. Only **flyeast** (1 of 8) burns a headline into the creative, and it is a 2019 ad. If the commissioner makes numbered text-on-image listicles, nothing in the A1 half of this batch is a peer — those are photo carousels whose words live outside the frame.

2. **Enumeration is almost never shown as a numeral.** Three A2 accounts promise a count (steveandnes "12", whatshappening365 "10" in caption, mustvisitjapan "7"), but only **1 of 4** (mustvisitjapan) actually renders a number badge on the item slide — a red "1." pill placed centrally. steveandnes labels its item with a bare phrase; whatshappening365 replaces numbering entirely with a repeating if/then sentence template. So the count is used as a **promise in the hook** far more often than as a **navigational marker on the slides**.

3. **The white/light highlight box is the dominant contrast method in organic decks, over scrims or outlines.** 3 of 4 A2 accounts put the text in an opaque light box or pill rather than on a scrim: steveandnes (white box, slide 1), whatshappening365 (two white boxes per slide), crete_travel_guide (rounded off-white card, slide 2). Only **mustvisitjapan** uses a bottom gradient scrim with the type sitting directly on the photo — and that is also the only A2 deck with a genuinely large headline and the highest engagement rate here (0.0234). Small text in a box is the norm; big text on a scrim is the exception.

4. **Slide 2 is used as the first item, not as a bridge.** In 3 of 4 A2 decks (steveandnes, whatshappening365, mustvisitjapan) slide 2 already delivers list item 1 — no "here's why", no agenda slide. Only crete_travel_guide uses slide 2 as a problem/solution turn because it is selling a guide, not a list. Correspondingly, explicit "keep swiping" art is rare: **1 of 4** (mustvisitjapan) draws an arrow; the rest rely on the number in the hook or on the template repeating.

5. **Two distinct A1 sequencing failures, from 2 of 8 advertisers.** בני ביטון leads with a grip-and-grin contract-signing snapshot and defers the actual product renders to slides 2-3; Miri Hamias leads with a magenta club selfie and defers the property to slide 2. Both bury the asset behind a personal/credential frame. By contrast, the 3 hospitality advertisers that lead with the asset (Pacifico Living, אחוזת הספא, Lear Sense) are also the three with long or ongoing run times (120, 128, 35 days) — an association, not a measurement, since baseline median runDays is null for every entry in this batch.

6. **No A1 deck in this batch has a closing slide.** All 8 ads simply stop on their last photo — no CTA card, no logo card, no price card, no "swipe up"/"link in bio" equivalent. The close is entirely the Meta CTA button. This includes the 5-slide deck (אחוזת הספא) and the 3-slide catalogue (flyeast), so it is not a function of deck length.

7. **Amateur/phone-shot imagery is common on the ad side and absent on the organic side.** 4 of 8 A1 advertisers use plainly amateur frames (בני ביטון's office snapshot, יוני פארג's salon phone shots, Miri Hamias's flash selfie, patric_palm's event selfie), while 3 use professional brand photography (Pacifico Living, אחוזת הספא, Lear Sense). All 4 A2 accounts use either consistent creator photography (steveandnes, crete_travel_guide) or clean stock/licensed imagery (whatshappening365, mustvisitjapan). Only one image in the whole batch reads as possibly AI-processed at a glance: patric_palm's cutout-on-orange headshot.

8. **Two entries are harvest false-positives** (יוני פארג' — a hair salon that happens to be located in a hotel; Miri Hamias — a villa listing described as "like a hotel"), and **two entries are the same creative** (ad-2100646697237787 and ad-1741755416948980, both Lear Sense). Effective distinct creatives in this batch: 11, of which 6 are genuinely travel/hospitality.
