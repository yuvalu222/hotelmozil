### ad-1237148664465229 — listicle — A1 — 9 slides — metric runDays=null, baseline advertiser median runDays=null
- **⭐ MAJOR EXCEPTION #1 — the strongest ad-side peer in this batch.** Kapawi Amazon Lodge. A fully designed, templated, text-on-image 9-slide narrative deck with a cover and a closing CTA card — it breaks three of the four wave-1 claims at once.

  - `A JOURNEY` / `TO KAPAWI` — dark forest-green, heavy geometric sans (Poppins/Montserrat-family, bold, wide tracking on caps), centred, two lines.
  - `Guied by indigenous hosts long before arrival` — **typo in the live creative, "Guied" for "Guided"** — same family, regular weight, dark green, sentence case.
  - `Stepping into the Amazon...` — italic, cream on a **dark-green rounded pill/knock-out bar** that straddles the seam between the cream panel and the photo. This pill is the deck's signature device and the only italic in the system.
  - Bottom of photo: centred `KAPAWI ECOLODGE` monoline logo, white, plus a **white right-arrow (→) glyph bottom-right = an explicit swipe affordance.** Rare on the ad side.

Body copy sits bottom-left (slides 2 & 5–8) or bottom-left-of-centre, always flush-left, 2–3 lines, **ALL-CAPS heavy sans, white, with a soft dark gradient scrim from the bottom edge** for contrast — no box, no bar, no outline. This is a lead-magnet end-card, i.e. the deck ends on CTA — directly contradicting the wave-1 "almost none ends on a CTA" claim.

## Batch-level observations

**Sample shape.** 12 decks, 12 distinct advertisers, 2 of them false-positives (megankgoldman — food creator; Crystal01 — crystal wholesaler), leaving **10 genuine travel/hospitality ads**. Two decks carry Ad Library duplicate-creative flags (Crystal01 "2 ads use this creative", GolfNow "8 ads use this creative"). Only one deck has any metric at all (Novotel, runDays=135); every baseline in the batch is null.

**Did the wave-1 claims hold?**

1. *"Almost every ad carries ZERO text on the creative; all copy lives in the caption."* — **Held for 10 of 12 decks** (National Parks of Japan, Kinyonga, Naturarte, Novotel, Anantara, Round Hill, Atlantica, GolfNow, plus both false-positives). **Contradicted by exactly 2: Kapawi Amazon Lodge and Rome2Rio** — both carry designed text on every single slide. That is a 17% exception rate, slightly higher than wave 1 implied, and both exceptions are in the "experience/product explainer" mode rather than the "room/property showcase" mode. The split is not random: **advertisers selling a property put zero text on the image; advertisers selling an understanding — a journey narrative, a how-to-get-around tool — build a text template.** That is the useful predictor for the commissioner.

2. *"Almost none ends on a CTA/logo/price slide."* — **Held for 11 of 12.** **Contradicted by Kapawi**, whose slide 9 is a full lead-magnet end-card with a solid cream button reading `DOWNLOAD THE GUIDE`. Rome2Rio does *not* end on a CTA; it ends on its last tip, so even one of the two designed decks declines the end-card. Notably Kapawi's end-card asks for an email, not a booking — the only deck in the batch that ends by asking for anything.

3. *"Emoji appear in captions, not on artwork."* — **Held in 12 of 12, with no exceptions whatsoever.** Several captions are saturated with them (National Parks of Japan 🐾🦦✨🥰, Rome2Rio ❄️🏔️🏙️🎿🚆, Kinyonga ➡️ ×5, GolfNow ⛳️ ×4, Naturarte 🇨🇷🇮🇹⚜️) and not one of those glyphs appears on a slide. Even the two fully-designed decks keep their artwork emoji-free — Rome2Rio's caption is the most emoji-dense in the batch and its slides are entirely emoji-free.

4. *"Aspect-ratio letterboxing (grey bars) is common on the ad side."* — **Held, and this batch is worse than the claim.** Visible letterboxing in **5 advertisers**: GolfNow (extreme — panoramic crops and inconsistent ratios between slides of the same deck), Novotel Jakarta, Atlantica Miramare, Anantara Tangalle, and mildly Rome2Rio. It clusters in hotel/venue advertisers repurposing existing landscape brand photography; neither Kapawi (native 1:1) nor Rome2Rio's cover suffers from it, because those were authored for the feed.

**Numbering: absent everywhere.** Neither designed deck uses a number, a counter, or an item label on-slide — 0 of 12 decks show "1/2/3" anywhere. Kapawi and Rome2Rio both achieve listicle legibility through **parallel grammar plus an identical recurring lockup** instead. For a Hebrew numbered-listicle maker this is the single most transferable finding in the batch: on the ad side, the number is not the convention; the *repeated identical container* is.

**Three distinct contrast devices, one per designer (3 advertisers):** (a) Kapawi — a solid cream panel on the cover plus a bottom gradient scrim with no box on interior slides, and one dark-green rounded pill as the signature accent; (b) Rome2Rio — an opaque black rounded caption pill pinned to the bottom edge on every interior slide, plus dark-on-blown-sky knockout on the cover; (c) Crystal01 (false-positive) — no text device, but a rigid 3×2 gutterless collage grid. No deck in the batch used an outline/stroke or a drop-shadow-on-text approach.

**Cover-slide convention among the designed decks (2 advertisers: Kapawi, Rome2Rio):** both build a distinct cover that does not match the interior template — a title lockup with a small all-caps kicker above a large bold title (`2026 TRAVEL GUIDE` / `Winter Olympics`; `A JOURNEY / TO KAPAWI` + subtitle). Both put the title in the **upper third**, both use a geometric/humanist bold sans, both centre it. Kapawi additionally draws an explicit **→ swipe arrow** — the only swipe affordance in the batch.

**Slide 2 behaviour (all 12):** slide 2 is almost never a hook-payoff. In the photo-only decks it is simply the next photograph — in 3 cases (Round Hill, Naturarte, megankgoldman) it adds essentially nothing the first slide did not already say, and Round Hill's slide 2 is literally slide 1 with the model removed. Only the 2 designed decks give slide 2 a job: Kapawi's first template slide establishes the format that the next six repeat; Rome2Rio's introduces the product screenshot that becomes the deck's anchor. Where a photo-only deck does use slide 2 well, it is a two-frame before/after (National Parks of Japan's winter/summer stoat) — and even there, without text, the payoff is only legible to someone who read the caption first.

**Price on creative: zero decks.** Kinyonga advertises five packages from $500 to $1150 and Atlantica/Novotel both push direct-booking rate deals; not one number appears on a slide. Rome2Rio is the only deck where prices are visible on-image at all, and only incidentally, inside its product screenshots (`US$61`, `US$50–174`).

**Provenance split (10 genuine travel decks):** professional/branded shoots in 7 (Japan parks, Kapawi, Novotel, Anantara, Round Hill, Atlantica, GolfNow), stock/licensed-or-lifted with visible inconsistency between slides in 1 (Kinyonga), authored-for-feed design over stock in 1 (Rome2Rio), and raw phone snaps in 1 (Naturarte). Two decks (Anantara, Round Hill) are visibly **video stills** rather than photographs — soft, motion-blurred, faces unresolvable. No CGI and no obvious AI generation anywhere in the batch.
