## Batch-level observations

Advertiser count below excludes the two duplicate IDs, so: **10 distinct advertisers, 12 ad IDs, 11 unique creatives** (Nomadic Anton ×2 IDs, amrachrononight ×2 IDs).

**Wave-1 claim 1 — "almost every travel ad carries ZERO text on the creative; all copy lives in the caption." HELD, and harder than wave 1 stated.**
- **10 of 10 advertisers** put zero copy on the photographic slides: Nomadic Anton, fastenupyourseatbelts, amrachrononight, Merzouga Desert Activities, kinyonga_tours_and_safaris, Design Hotels, Falkensteiner Punta Skala, InterContinental New York Barclay, Luxury Deals 4 U, The Holiday Dealers.
- The single on-image text in the whole batch is **one word** — the "Chrononight" wordmark on a synthetic brand card (amrachrononight), and that advertiser is a **non-travel false positive**. So among the nine genuine travel/hospitality advertisers here, **on-image text does not occur at all.**
- **Sharpest finding: four advertisers wrote a fully structured, numbered or priced listicle — into the caption.** Merzouga (Day 1 / Day 2 itinerary with distances and named stops), kinyonga (an 8-line route × duration price table), Luxury Deals 4 U (four price tiers plus deposit), amrachrononight (a literal "1. 2. 3. 4." tip list). Every one of them had the content for a numbered text-on-image deck and shipped photos instead. The constraint is evidently habit or production capacity, not lack of material.

**Wave-1 claim 2 — "almost none ends on a CTA/logo/price slide." HELD, with no exceptions at all.**
- **10 of 10 advertisers** end on an ordinary photograph. Zero end-cards, zero logo slides, zero price slides, zero "Book now" artwork — including the three whose entire pitch is a discount (Design Hotels "Up to 30% Off", Luxury Deals 4 U £5180→£4850, kinyonga $1300–$2000). Not one number reached a pixel.

**Wave-1 claim 3 — "emoji appear in captions, not on artwork." HELD, 10 of 10.**
- Nine advertisers use emoji in caption only (Nomadic Anton 📍📸✈️🏋️🧳, fastenupyourseatbelts ✈️💑💕🧳🎯, Merzouga 🌅🗓, kinyonga ➡️, Falkensteiner 🌞🌴🧳✈️, InterContinental 📷, Luxury Deals 😍🙌❤️👀⭐️, Holiday Dealers 🌿🦁✨🚙✅🦓🏕️, amrachrononight •→). **Zero on-slide emoji anywhere in the batch.**
- Design Hotels is the lone emoji-free advertiser, caption included — and it is also the most designed deck. Suggestive, on n=1.

**Wave-1 claim 4 — "aspect-ratio letterboxing (grey bars) is common on the ad side." HELD, and it is the dominant defect: 8 of 10 advertisers.**
- Grey bars in: Nomadic Anton (side bars), amrachrononight (side bars, slide 2), Merzouga (side bars, 3 of 5), Falkensteiner (top+bottom, all 7, ~40% of tile), InterContinental (side bars), Luxury Deals 4 U (top+bottom, all 8, up to ~60% of tile — worst in batch), The Holiday Dealers (top+bottom, all 4), kinyonga (thin bars).
- Only **fastenupyourseatbelts** and **Design Hotels** deliver full-bleed frames that fit the slot. Notably these are the two ends of the quality range — a phone-snapping creator couple and a luxury hotel brand — which says the fix is a cropping decision, not a budget.

**New pattern not in wave 1 — the "borrowed asset" tell (4 of 10 advertisers).** Luxury Deals 4 U (Sandals brand library), The Holiday Dealers (licensed/lodge wildlife), Falkensteiner (agency shoot), InterContinental (a creator's own fashion shoot, credited). In each case the advertiser is republishing someone else's frames, which explains both the letterboxing (masters cut for a different slot) and the absence of type (no one owns the file to edit it). The two advertisers who *shot their own* — Merzouga and kinyonga — produced the weakest frames but the only ones matching their own offer.

**New pattern — video frames used as carousel slides (2 of 10 advertisers):** kinyonga (both slides) and fastenupyourseatbelts (slide 1). Soft, smeared, flat-contrast stills pulled from footage. Visibly degraded next to the neighbouring photographs.

**New pattern — slide 2 is almost never a step forward (9 of 10 advertisers).** Slide 2 is a second sample of the same idea (another portrait, another beach, another pose) rather than a new beat. The only advertiser whose slide 2 advances an argument is **Design Hotels** (landscape → architecture → interior → detail), and it does so with photography alone.

**AI/CGI provenance:** one advertiser, **amrachrononight** — slide 1 is a generated vector/wireframe brand card and slide 2 reads as AI-generated or heavily AI-retouched photography. No other deck shows AI tells.

**Contradictions to wave 1: none.** No deck in this batch contradicts any of the four claims. The nearest thing to an exception, amrachrononight's wordmark card, is (a) a single word of brand furniture rather than a hook or an item label, (b) on a synthetic field with no contrast device at all, (c) not repeated on slide 2 — no template, no grid, no numbering — and (d) from a **non-travel advertiser**. **This batch supplies no ad-side peer for a numbered, templated, text-on-image Hebrew listicle.**
