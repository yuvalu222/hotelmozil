### ad-2401324727007268 — listicle — A1 — 4 slides — metric runDays=null, baseline advertiser median runDays=null
- Advertiser **YumTravel** (France home-dining host community, €19/year membership).
- **🟢 THE MAJOR EXCEPTION OF THIS BATCH — and the single closest ad-side peer in it to a numbered Hebrew text-on-image listicle.** Every one of the four slides is a fully designed, templated, text-on-image composition. It carries copy on the creative, it carries brand on the creative, and it **ends on a hard CTA/logo/URL slide**. It contradicts three of the four wave-1 claims simultaneously.
- **Shared template across all four slides (this is what makes it a deck rather than four posters):**
  - **Ground:** a pale off-white **graph-paper / squared-notebook texture**, identical on all four slides, full bleed. This is the deck's single strongest consistency device — you can tell slide 3 belongs to slide 1 from a thumbnail, with no logo visible.
  - **Contrast device:** two different ones, used deliberately. On slide 1 the hook sits in an **opaque orange knock-out bar** — a full-width solid rectangle of brand orange with white text reversed out, laid straight across the middle of the illustration so it clears the artwork underneath. On slides 2–4 there is **no bar at all**: the type sits directly on the bare paper ground, where it needs no scrim. So the bar is used only where a photo/graphic would otherwise fight the type. That is the correct, disciplined choice and worth copying.
  - **Type system — exactly two families, in fixed roles:**
    - A **blue handwritten script** (informal marker-pen character, connected letterforms, slight upward baseline drift) always used for the *top line / voice line* — "Meet local hosts with Yum Travel", "The real France for just 19€/year.", "Click below to sign up…". Ink colour is the deep navy of the brand mark.
    - A **heavy geometric sans, bold, tight leading** (Poppins/Montserrat-family character, round bowls, near-circular o) always used for the *claim / benefit* lines, set in brand orange — or reversed to white when it sits inside the orange bar.
    - Rule the deck never breaks: **script = the brand talking, bold sans = the offer.** Colour reinforces it: navy script vs orange sans.
  - **Alignment:** all text is **centred**, on every slide, always full-width of the column. No left rag anywhere.
  - **Brand lockup:** the circular orange/navy spoon-and-fork roundel is parked **bottom-centre** on slides 1, 2 and 3 at a small, identical size — a footer mark, not a headline. On slide 4 the same roundel is promoted to hero size at the centre, at full detail with its circular ring text ("Each meal is a journey" / "Each home, a destination"). Same asset, two scales, and that scale change alone signals "this is the last slide".
- **Slide-1 hook text VERBATIM:** *"Tired of tourist traps and overpriced menus?"* / *"Discover France through our local hosts."* (two lines, white on the orange bar; line 1 is the problem, line 2 is the promise).
  - Slide 1's graphic is a **flat navy silhouette map of France** centred on the paper, with four **Polaroid-style photo cards** — white border, slight random rotation, handwritten-script caption on the bottom lip — tacked at the four corners: *"Oyster"*, *"Quiche Lorraine"*, *"Boeuf Bourguignon"*, *"French cheese"*. Each Polaroid is joined to the map by a **hand-drawn curved arrow** in the same navy. This is a labelled-item layout — the nearest thing here to a numbered list, except the items are named rather than numbered.
- **Slide 2** is the mechanism/how-it-works beat: script headline, bold-sans claim, then a **2×2-ish photo mosaic** (three warm-lit dinner-table photos in rounded-corner tiles, one spanning the full right column), then a second bold-sans line beneath — *"Discover the heart of French hospitality."* Type-above / grid / type-below is the deck's core lockup.
- **Slide 3** repeats slide 2's lockup **exactly** — script top line, photo mosaic of the same shape, bold-sans benefit block below — and drops the price into the script line. That literal repetition is what makes the deck feel templated rather than assembled.
- **Colour treatment:** the photos are all warm-toned, soft-daylight, mild-contrast lifestyle stock, deliberately kept low-saturation so the orange type stays the loudest thing on the slide. The palette is exactly three inks — brand orange, brand navy, paper white — plus a magenta that appears only once, on the URL.
- **On-slide emoji: none.** Even this deck keeps emoji out of the artwork — the caption carries 🇫🇷🏠✨✅. So the wave-1 emoji claim survives even the exception.
- **Ending — the strongest contradiction of wave-1 in this batch:** slide 4 is a **pure CTA card with no photograph at all**. Stack, top to bottom: bold-sans orange question *"Ready for a meal you'll never forget ?"* → three centred lines of navy script instruction → a hand-drawn curved arrow pointing down-right into → the hero roundel logo → the URL **"yum-travel.com"** set in bold sans in **magenta**, a colour used nowhere else in the deck purely to make the click target the loudest pixel → the handle *"@yumtravelcommunity"* in orange → a row of three social glyphs (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn). CTA + logo + handle + URL, all on one slide.
- Caption carries the price again (€19/year) and ✅-bulleted benefits, so the offer is stated on-image *and* in caption — belt and braces, unlike every other deck here.

## Batch-level observations

**How the wave-1 claims held in this batch**

1. **"Almost every travel ad carries ZERO text on the creative; all copy lives in the caption."** — **Held, 11 of 12 decks.** Text-free creative appeared across **10 different advertisers**: Trip.Explore, Friendly Shandong, Anhui Travel, mariana_cy, rayjaycab, Elden Ubud View Private Villas, Round Hill Hotel & Villas, Anantara Peace Haven Tangalle, GolfNow UK & Ireland (both decks), and loveinfirstclass.co on its photo slide.
   - **Contradicted outright by one deck: `ad-2401324727007268` (YumTravel)** — every slide is text-on-image.
   - **Partially contradicted by `ad-820827186961382` (loveinfirstclass.co)** — a designed wordmark card, but it is a logo, not copy, and it avoids the photo-legibility problem by having no photo.
   - **Near-miss worth naming: `ad-1304989148183492` / `ad-747594641614254` (GolfNow, 1 advertiser, 2 decks).** These prove the pattern is a *placement* choice, not a *content* one: GolfNow does write per-slide item labels — including a numbered one — but puts them in Meta's link-card chrome instead of on the pixels. So "no text on the creative" does not always mean "no per-slide labels".

2. **"Almost none ends on a CTA/logo/price slide."** — **Held, 11 of 12.** Ten advertisers end on an undifferentiated photograph; several end badly (Elden Ubud ends on a bathroom; rayjaycab ends on an empty branch after its people have already disappeared).
   - **Contradicted by exactly one: YumTravel**, whose slide 4 is a photo-free CTA card carrying question + instruction + hero logo + URL + handle + three social icons.
   - **Inverted by one: loveinfirstclass.co**, which puts its brand card **first** and ends on the photo.

3. **"Emoji appear in captions, not on artwork."** — **Held, 12 of 12, with zero exceptions.** Not one on-slide emoji anywhere in the batch, including on the fully-designed YumTravel deck. GolfNow's ⛳️ is in link-card chrome, not on the image. Captions meanwhile are dense with them (🏁✨🔥, 🌸💡, 🇫🇷🏠✅, 🌸🇯🇵, ✨, 😊😄🤓, 👉).

4. **"Aspect-ratio letterboxing (grey bars) is common on the ad side."** — **Held and then some: 8 of 12 decks, across 7 advertisers** (Friendly Shandong, Anhui Travel, Elden Ubud, Round Hill, Anantara, mariana_cy, GolfNow ×2). Two failure flavours worth separating:
   - *Consistent* letterboxing — same ratio every slide (Anhui Travel, Elden Ubud, Anantara). Reads as a format mismatch but not as carelessness.
   - *Inconsistent* letterboxing — the bar size changes slide to slide inside one deck (Friendly Shandong, both GolfNow decks, and Trip.Explore which varies its crop without bars). This is worse: the image visibly jumps size as you swipe. **No deck in this batch was designed at the slot's native ratio except YumTravel**, which is the only one authored as slides rather than sourced as photos.

**Other repeated patterns**

- **Deck-order is unmanaged — 9 decks, 8 advertisers.** Slides are interchangeable; removing or reordering any middle slide would change nothing. Only Anhui Travel (process order: raw → shaping → finished) and YumTravel (problem → mechanism → price → CTA) have an argued sequence. **Slide 2 is almost never a distinct beat** — in 9 of 12 decks it is simply "another photo of the same thing".
- **Literal duplication inside a deck — 1 advertiser (rayjaycab):** slides 2 and 3 are the same setup at slightly different crops, and slides 4–6 drop the subject entirely.
- **The offer is invisible on the artwork — 4 advertisers** (Elden Ubud: a nightly rate; Round Hill: a free fourth night; rayjaycab: an open 2026 calendar and a "DM BLOOM" prompt; loveinfirstclass.co: a follow). In all four, the ad's entire commercial ask exists only in caption text that a scrolling viewer may never expand. **YumTravel is the only advertiser in the batch whose price is legible on the image itself** ("The real France for just 19€/year").
- **Watermark-as-branding — 1 advertiser (rayjaycab):** a translucent script signature, applied to 5 of 6 slides and missing from slide 1. It brands the photo but communicates nothing and, unlike YumTravel's footer roundel, is not placed consistently.
- **First-party vs stock split:** genuinely first-party imagery in 6 advertisers (Anhui Travel, rayjaycab, Elden Ubud, Round Hill, Anantara, mariana_cy). Licensed/stock-feeling in 3 (Trip.Explore, Friendly Shandong, and YumTravel's mosaic photos). No CGI and no obvious AI-generated imagery anywhere in this batch.
- **Phone-snap provenance — 1 advertiser (mariana_cy),** and it is the only deck where amateur capture is clearly deliberate (a creator-intro ad where a polished shoot would undercut the "hi, I'm a real person" premise).

**Duplicates and false positives**

- **Duplicate creatives sharing one advertiser: `ad-1304989148183492` and `ad-747594641614254` are both GolfNow UK & Ireland**, same four-card template, identical slide 4 photograph and identical cards 3–4 headlines. The first also carries the library note "4 ads use this creative and text", so the true creative count behind these two entries is lower than the entry count. The `account` field on ad-1304989148183492 is **null** in the batch file and had to be recovered from the caption — worth fixing in the harvest.
- **No non-travel false positives.** All 12 advertisers are travel, hospitality or travel-adjacent services. Two borderline cases, both legitimately travel-owned: **Anhui Travel** (a tourism board, but this deck sells a calligraphy brush, not a trip) and **rayjaycab** (a destination-photography service rather than a travel seller). **GolfNow** is tee-time booking — travel-adjacent commerce, correctly in scope.

**The one takeaway for a numbered Hebrew text-on-image listicle**

Only **YumTravel** is a real ad-side peer, and the transferable parts are narrow and specific: (a) a **single constant background texture** carries deck identity better than a repeated logo; (b) **two type families in permanently fixed roles** — script for voice, heavy sans for the claim — with colour reinforcing the split; (c) the **opaque knock-out bar is used only on the one slide where type sits over artwork**, and dropped everywhere else, rather than applied reflexively; (d) the brand mark stays a small bottom-centre footer for the whole deck and is **promoted to hero scale only on the final slide**, which signals the ending without a word; (e) the **CTA slide drops photography entirely** and reserves one otherwise-unused colour (magenta) for the URL alone. Everything else in this batch is a photo roll with the argument hidden in the caption.
