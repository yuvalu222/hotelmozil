# 04 — Why people save

The metric we optimise. On Photo Mode the save rate reaches ~5%, about ten
times typical video, and saves are treated by the ranker as a high-value
signal because they imply intent to return.

## The sharpest finding of the night so far

`[LIT]` **People do not save what they already know.** They will not revisit
what they have already seen, done or tried. A save is a bet that the content
will be useful again, later.

This is a hard test I can apply to every single line we write:

> Does this slide tell an Israeli traveller something they do not already know?

Run against our own Dubai deck, much of it fails:
- "המטבע הוא דירהם" — everyone knows, or finds out in ten seconds
- "eSIM זה הכי פשוט" — generic, true of everywhere
- "כרטיס אשראי מתקבל כמעט בכל מקום" — assumed
- "וואי פיי יש כמעט בכל מקום" — assumed

And what passes:
- "יש תרופות נפוצות שאסורות להכניס" — genuinely not known, and costly
- "לא להחליף כסף בשדה התעופה" — known to some, worth the reminder
- "תעלו שני רחובות פנימה, חצי מחיר" — specific and actionable

`[LIT]` The second driver is **emotional**: anxiety about losing something
valuable in an infinite feed. That is why "save this before your trip" works
as an instruction — it names the loss.

`[LIT]` A like is near-automatic and costs nothing; a save requires the viewer
to stop and judge the content worth keeping. So save rate is a cleaner quality
signal than like rate, which is why the corpus was ranked on saves per like
throughout rather than on likes.

## What this implies for us, not yet acted on

- Every slide should be auditable against "do they already know this?".
  A deck that is 60% known facts is a deck nobody saves.
- Specific beats general: a named street, a price, an hour, a rule.
- The guides (tt-1, tt-3, tt-5) carry the most known-fact filler and are the
  decks most exposed to this.
