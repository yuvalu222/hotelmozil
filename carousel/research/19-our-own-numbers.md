# 19 — Measuring our own posts

## A correction to my own advice
I told him that getting his own analytics was the single most important action
available, because every number in this log is someone else's.

`[LIT]` **TikTok does not expose a swipe-through or completion rate for
carousels.** Story-style completion is not surfaced. The strongest signal in
the format is the one the creator cannot see.

So the advice stands but the method changes.

## What IS visible, and what to do with it
From the native dashboard: views, likes, comments, shares, saves.

The one computable metric with a published benchmark:

| save rate (saves ÷ views) | reading |
| --- | --- |
| below 3% | under par |
| **3%+** | healthy |
| **5%+** | exceptional; `[LIT]` reported to trigger wider distribution |

`[LIT]` Comments per view above **0.5%** is the healthy mark for carousels.

`[LIT]` Benchmarks for the invisible metric, for reference only: a healthy
completion rate is 40-60% on an 8+ slide deck, and a strong first slide moves
60%+ of viewers to slide two.

## How to infer what cannot be measured
Completion has to be triangulated:
- **saves ÷ views** is the closest proxy we have, because a save usually
  happens at or near the end.
- **A/B by length.** Post the same kind of deck at 7 slides and at 12 and
  compare save rate. With his library that is a real experiment, not a guess.
- **shares ÷ views** moves with perceived completeness.

## The one experiment worth running first
Everything in `00-THE-SPEC.md` is measured on English-language accounts with
large followings. Nothing is measured on a 2,000-follower Hebrew account.

The cheapest way to find out whether any of it transfers:
1. Post the existing decks on a fixed daily cadence.
2. Record views, saves, shares per post in a simple sheet.
3. After ~15 posts, correlate save rate against slide count and caption length
   — the two variables the corpus says matter most.

That turns this whole log from borrowed knowledge into his own data, and it is
the only thing that can.
