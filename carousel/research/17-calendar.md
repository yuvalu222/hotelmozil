# 17 — When to post what

## The Israeli travel calendar
`[DATA]` School holiday windows, which drive family travel:

| window | dates | length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rosh Hashanah 2026 | 11-13 Sep | 3 days |
| Yom Kippur + Sukkot 2026 | **20 Sep - 3 Oct** | ~2 unbroken weeks |
| Hanukkah 2026 | **6-12 Dec** | 7 days |
| Passover 2027 | **14-28 Apr** | **15 days** |
| Shavuot 2027 | 10-11 Jun | 2 days |
| Summer | **July - August** | **62 days** |

`[DATA]` September 2026 was almost entirely vacation for Israeli schools —
Yom Kippur, the days after, and Suktot running straight on.

## The timing rule this implies
`[DATA]` Bookings in the **first week of the summer school break doubled**
against the weekly average, and August 2026 set the all-time record at 1.4
million departures. `[LIT]` Demand also peaks around the autumn holidays,
Sukkot especially.

So the pattern is: **demand spikes inside the window, but the booking — and
therefore the research, and therefore the saving — happens before it.** Content
has to lead the window, not sit inside it.

## Where that puts us right now
As of **5 October 2026** the Sukkot window has just closed. The next windows
are Hanukkah (6-12 Dec) and then the big one, Passover (14-28 Apr 2027,
fifteen days).

A reasonable lead time, not yet validated against data: start posting for a
window **4-6 weeks ahead**, which puts Hanukkah content from late October and
Passover content from early March.

**I have not found data on how far ahead Israelis actually book.** That is the
missing number and it would set the lead time properly. Flagged, not guessed.
