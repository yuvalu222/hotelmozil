# 09 — Travel, specifically

## The strongest triangulation in this log

Three independent lines point at the same thing:

1. `[CORPUS]` The two highest saves-per-like decks of 524, from two different
   accounts, are **itinerary infographics with hard numbers** — ticket price,
   opening hours, walking time between stops.
2. `[LIT]` *"Detailed itineraries with price breakdowns are save-friendly
   because viewers treat them as trip-planning resources, including specific
   restaurant names, hotel costs and transport tips."*
3. `[LIT]` People do not save what they already know (see `04-saves.md`). A
   price, an opening hour and a street name are precisely what they do not
   know.

When measurement, outside research and psychology converge, that is as close to
settled as this log gets.

## The emotional driver
`[LIT]` **Pre-trip anxiety drives saves.** Someone with a flight booked
consumes this content obsessively. That is the state to write for — not the
daydreamer, the person who has already paid and is now afraid of getting it
wrong.

It also explains why mistake decks work: they speak directly to that fear.

## Angles the research names as high performing
- **Cost breakdowns** — daily spend, named prices
- **Hidden gems with a budget angle** — the combination, not either alone
- **Secondary cities** — the second or third city of a country: similar
  architecture and food, half the crowds, much lower prices
- **Credibility markers** — naming transport, quieter seasons, and respect for
  residents reads as someone who actually went

`[CORPUS]` note: our `tt-13` (אותו מראה, חצי מחיר) is the secondary-city angle
already, built before I read any of this.
