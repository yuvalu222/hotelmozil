# 05 — The cover, and how frames change down a deck

## Covers, measured on 524 of them `[CORPUS]`

Top quartile against bottom, cover frame only:

| | top | bottom | separation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **text band height** | 0.208 | 0.060 | **+0.72 sd** |
| text coverage | 0.0160 | 0.0090 | +0.44 sd |
| rows of text | 3.0 | 2.0 | +0.38 sd |
| busyness | 0.111 | 0.090 | +0.36 sd |
| contrast | 56.6 | 53.5 | +0.27 sd |
| saturation | 0.335 | 0.308 | +0.19 sd |
| colourfulness | 45.3 | 42.0 | +0.14 sd |
| dark pixels | 0.230 | 0.237 | -0.03 sd |

### The single clearest result in the log
**Covers with no text at all: 7 of 131 in the top quartile, 35 of 131 in the
bottom.** A five-fold difference. A wordless cover is the strongest marker of
a deck nobody saves.

### What the cover needs, from the numbers
- **Text, always.** Never a bare photograph.
- **A block, not a line.** Three rows beats two; band height is the strongest
  single signal at +0.72 sd.
- Type and composition beat colour here. Every colour metric is under +0.2 sd
  on covers, while the text-structure metrics are 0.4-0.7.

This does not contradict the colour research in `10-colour`: that research
compares photographs across a full quality range, while this compares covers
that all already cleared 50,000 likes. Within elite covers, what separates them
is the typography, not the picture.

## Caveat kept in view
All of this is association, not cause. Decks with a strong text block on the
cover are probably also decks made by people who think harder about
everything. The direction is consistent and large; the mechanism is assumed.
