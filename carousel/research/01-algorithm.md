# 01 — How Photo Mode is ranked

## The four signals
`[LIT]` swipe-through rate · dwell time · reverse swipes · **completion rate**,
with completion rate the primary one. If a viewer reaches the last slide,
TikTok counts a completed view and widens distribution. The ranker actively
penalises carousels that lose a large share of viewers after slide one or two.

## Why the format is worth the effort at all
`[LIT]` TikTok's own figures: carousels take **1.9x the likes, 2.9x the
comments, 2.6x the shares** of video. An independent analysis of 700,000 posts
found 81% higher engagement. Photo Mode save rates reach ~5%, roughly ten times
typical video.

## Numeric targets to aim at
`[LIT]` These are the first concrete numbers I have for what "good" means:

| metric | healthy | exceptional |
| --- | --- | --- |
| slide 1 -> 2 swipe-through | 60%+ | |
| full completion | 80%+ | |
| completion on an 8+ slide deck | 40-60% | |
| save rate | 3%+ | 5%+ triggers wider distribution |

We cannot see these numbers for our own posts without the creator analytics,
which is a gap worth closing — they are the only real feedback loop.

## Converging evidence on deck length
Two independent sources agree, which is the strongest thing in this log:

| source | finding |
| --- | --- |
| `[CORPUS]` 524 decks, saves/like | 6-13 slides best (0.50), 17-20 falls (0.32), 21+ collapses (0.17) |
| `[LIT]` practitioner data | 5-10 optimal, completion drops sharply past 10, under 5 starves dwell time |

Stated in the source almost exactly as the owner put it: *35 mediocre slides
will perform worse than 7 great ones.*
