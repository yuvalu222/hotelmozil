# 20 — Length and density interact, and density wins

`[CORPUS]` 524 decks crossed on two axes: slide count, and the deck's median
text coverage per slide. Cells under 10 decks suppressed.

**median saves/like**

| | sparse | medium | dense |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **short 4-8** | 0.36 (n=88) | 0.50 (n=37) | **0.60 (n=78)** |
| **mid 9-13** | 0.38 (n=99) | **0.57 (n=45)** | 0.49 (n=57) |
| **long 14+** | **0.20 (n=74)** | 0.37 (n=26) | 0.55 (n=20) |

Corpus median 0.44.

## Three readings

**1. Density beats length.** In every length band, denser outperforms sparser.
The effect of density within a band (0.36 → 0.60 for short decks) is larger
than the effect of length within a density band.

**2. The best cell is short AND dense — 0.60 on 78 decks.** That is a healthy
sample, not a fluke, and it is 36% above the corpus median.

**3. The worst cell by a distance is long AND sparse — 0.20 on 74 decks.**
Less than half the median.

## This corrects section 9a
Earlier I wrote that decks past 17 slides "collapse to 0.17". More precisely:
**long and sparse collapses.** Long and dense reaches 0.55 (n=20, small — treat
as suggestive), almost level with short and dense.

So the operative rule is not *cut slides*. It is **never carry a sparse
slide.** Cutting is simply the easiest way to guarantee that, and it has the
independent benefit of raising completion rate.

Restated for use:

> Every slide earns its place by carrying real content. If a slide cannot be
> made dense, it should not exist. A deck of seven dense slides and a deck of
> fifteen dense slides both work; a deck with sparse slides anywhere does not.

This is the owner's "כל אחת פיצוץ" stated as a measurement, and it explains why
his instinct to cut four slides from the Dubai deck was right — he was removing
the sparse ones.

## Caveat
Text coverage is a proxy for "carries content". A slide could be dense with
worthless text. Nothing here measures whether the words are any good.
