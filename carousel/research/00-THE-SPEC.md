# The spec — everything the night produced, in one page

Written 5-6.10.2026. Every line traces to a file in this folder. Nothing here
was applied to a slide; the owner asked for learning only.

Confidence is marked: **[strong]** = measured on 524 decks AND confirmed by an
outside source · **[measured]** = corpus only · **[read]** = outside source
only · **[weak]** = small sample or practitioner opinion.

---

## 1. Deck length
**6 to 11 slides. Never more than 13.** [strong]
- `[CORPUS]` 6-13 median 0.50 saves/like; 17-20 falls to 0.32; **21+ collapses
  to 0.17** against a 0.44 corpus median.
- `[LIT]` Independent practitioner data: 5-10 optimal, completion drops sharply
  past 10.
- `[CORPUS]` All **fourteen** accounts with a repeatable system sit in 6-11.
  Not one runs long.
- The decks that get more saves than likes median **7** slides.

## 1b. ...but density is the real rule  [measured]
`[CORPUS]` Crossing length against density (`20-length-x-density.md`):
short+dense **0.60**, long+sparse **0.20**, long+dense 0.55. Density beats
length in every band. The operative rule is **never carry a sparse slide** —
cutting is just the easiest way to guarantee it.

## 2. Text density
**Far more text than we have been putting on a slide.** [strong]
- `[CORPUS]` Elite decks (saves > likes) carry **0.0180** text coverage and
  **5.8 rows** per slide, against 0.0056 and 2.0 for everyone else — **3x**.
- `[CORPUS]` The gap holds at **every position** in the deck, not just the cover.
- This reverses the obvious reading of "too much text". Density was never the
  problem. A single line floating over a photograph is.

## 3. The deck must not fade
**The last fifth is as dense as the first.** [measured]
- `[CORPUS]` Top quartile: 0.0141 → 0.0128 across the deck, flat.
  Bottom quartile busyness decays 0.095 → 0.082.
- Mechanism: completion rate is the primary ranking signal `[read]`, so a deck
  that weakens loses viewers before the closing pair.

## 4. The cover
**Always text. A block, never a line.** [measured]
- `[CORPUS]` Covers with no text: **7/131 top quartile vs 35/131 bottom** — 5x.
- Text band height is the strongest cover signal, +0.72 sd. Every colour
  metric is under +0.2 sd.
- `[read]` Slide one decides ~80% of swipe-through and has ~1.3 seconds.
  8-12 words.

## 5. The last slide
**Full, worded, high in frame.** [measured]
- `[CORPUS]` Top: 0.90x the deck's own density, 4 rows, starts at 0.245.
  Bottom: 0.58x, 1 row, and **47/125 have no text at all**.
- His existing closing pair already matches the top profile.

## 6. The caption
**Write one, and ask for the save.** [strong]
- `[CORPUS]` Tags only 0.32 · 12-24 words 0.53 · 60+ words 0.61.
- `[CORPUS]` "save" appears in **31% of top-third captions, 5% of bottom** — 6x.
- `[CORPUS]` Hashtag COUNT does nothing (0.42 / 0.49 / 0.44 vs 0.44 median).
- `[read]` A question in the caption earns **+26.19% comments** (n=2.31M).
- `[CORPUS]` Top vocabulary is concrete planning: places 22x, itinerary 15x,
  free 11x, hours 9x, spots 7x. No beauty adjectives.

## 7. What to make
**Itineraries and costs with hard numbers.** [strong — triangulated]
- `[CORPUS]` The two highest saves-per-like decks, from different accounts, are
  both itinerary infographics with price, hours and walking time.
- `[read]` "Detailed itineraries with price breakdowns are save-friendly."
- `[read]` People **do not save what they already know.** Test every line.
- `[read]` Pre-trip anxiety is the driver. Write for someone who has already
  paid and is afraid of getting it wrong — which is also why mistake decks work.

## 8. The picture
**Saturation first.** [read]
- `[LIT]` Saturation is the single most critical attribute viewers respond to
  in travel imagery, ahead of caption style, hue and brightness. Orange,
  yellow, blue and violet predict popularity. A colourful sunset genuinely does
  beat a flat one.
- `[CORPUS]` Within the elite corpus colour separates weakly — but that is
  restriction of range, not evidence against it. See `10-corrections.md`.

## 9. Cadence
**Daily beats weekly by a wide margin.** [read]
- Daily vs 2-3x week: **3.5x follower growth**. 3+ per week: 2.5x monthly
  growth. Accounts under 2,000 followers average just 2 posts a week.
- 25 finished decks at one a day is a month of posting.

## 10. Timing
**Lead the window.** [read]
- Next Israeli windows: Hanukkah **6-12 Dec 2026**, Passover **14-28 Apr 2027**
  (15 days), summer July-August (62 days).
- Booking happens before the window; content must precede it. **Unknown:** how
  far ahead Israelis actually book. That number would set the lead time.

## 11. Destinations
**Greece, Cyprus, UAE, Italy — and Egypt, which we have nothing for.** [read]
- Top flight destinations from TLV: Greece, USA, UAE, Cyprus, Italy.
  **Egypt is the most visited overall; Cyprus the most visited in Europe.**
- Our library: six Greece decks, one Cyprus, zero Egypt.

---

## The three things that would change the most, if acted on
1. **Cut every deck to 6-11 slides and triple the text on each.** Two decks are
   at 21 — the worst band in the corpus.
2. **Write a real caption on every post and ask for the save.** Currently
   tags-only, which is the 0.32 group.
3. **Post daily.** The library already holds a month of content.

## What is still unknown
- How far ahead Israelis book — sets the posting lead time.
- Whether any of the English-corpus caption findings transfer to Hebrew.
- Our own analytics: completion rate, swipe-through, save rate per post. Without
  them every number here is someone else's.
