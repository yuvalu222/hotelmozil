# 02 — Slide one

## How much rides on it
`[PRACTICE]` Roughly **80% of swipe-through rate** is decided by slide one, and
it has about **1.3 seconds** to stop a thumb. Keep it **under ten words**.

## The mechanism
Every hook that works opens an information gap: it states enough to create
tension and withholds the resolution, so the only way to close the loop is to
swipe. The families that recur across sources:

- **withheld specific** — name the result, hide the method
- **contradiction** — assert that a common belief is wrong, then redirect
- **insider framing** — "nobody tells you that..."
- **before and after** — I did X, changed one thing, got Y
- **numbered preview with a callout** — "seven mistakes, the fourth is the worst"
- **cost framing** — name what the mistake costs

`[LIT]` Curiosity-gap and before/after hooks outperform the other shapes.

So the decks built on mistakes already have the strongest covers, and the
"list of nice things" decks have the weakest. That lines up with his note that
negative content is viral content, and with the corpus, where the
mistakes/`❌` shape recurs at the top.
