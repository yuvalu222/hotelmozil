# 02 - What practitioners with real data say about titles, hooks and cover text

Written 10.10.2026. Scope: YouTube titles/thumbnails, TikTok hooks, Instagram/TikTok
carousels, email subject lines, and the copywriting classics. Every claim carries a
source URL, a verbatim quote, and a grade. Primary sources were read directly wherever
possible (MrBeast PDF, four YouTube transcripts, three peer-reviewed/working papers,
the full text of Caples, Ogilvy and Schwartz). Where only a secondary summary could be
reached, the line says so.

## Grading key

| grade | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **A** | Tested with data: randomized A/B or split test, effect measured (Upworthy RCTs, Caples keyed-coupon tests, YouTube Test & Compare) |
| **B** | Large observational dataset (thousands to millions of items), correlational; or a platform reporting its own aggregate data without publishing n |
| **C** | Anecdote / self-report from a big creator or operator (real numbers, single channel, not controlled) |
| **D** | Opinion, theory, or guidance without published data |

Two warnings that apply to everything below:
1. **Almost all evidence is English-language.** Transfer to Hebrew covers is an assumption (D) until checked against our own census (`census-tables.md`, `04-hebrew-voice.md`).
2. **Most "title" research is about a title that sits next to an image.** A TikTok/Instagram carousel cover is title and thumbnail fused into one frame, so YouTube *thumbnail-text* findings (very short) matter at least as much as *title* findings.

### 1.4 Veritasium, "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective" (2021)

### 1.6 Large observational title studies

**vidIQ, 4M+ videos uploaded in 2026** - https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-title-formulas-study/ (page read in full). Grade **B**; the page itself says "The study is observational ... the lift figures are descriptive rather than causal estimates" and per-frame counts were not published.

| title frame | reported vs average | vidIQ's label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caught on camera | "More than 4x average" | Witnessed footage |
| Turning X into Y | "More than 3x" | Visible transformation |
| Nobody / no one knows | "More than 2.5x" | Insider knowledge |
| Inside X | "2.5x" | Hidden access |
| Age or tenure | "More than 2x" | Earned credibility |
| Banned, forbidden, illegal | "More than 2x" | Restricted access |
| Do not, stop, never | "Nearly 2x" | Warning and loss avoidance |
| How X did a surprising thing | "Nearly 2x" | Narrative payoff |
| Specific superlative | "More than 1.5x" | Meaningful extreme |
| X days, hours, or years | "Nearly 1.5x" | Bounded journey |
| **Question at the end** | "0.82 times average, or about 18% below average" | - |
| **I-was-wrong confession** | "below half of average" | - |
| Plain how-to | "1.07 times" | - |

Also: "The largest cited outliers combined three or four frames in one coherent idea."

**1of10, 300,000+ videos from 2025 (62.6B views, 52,000 channels)** - https://1of10.com/blog/what-actually-makes-a-youtube-video-go-viral-in-2025/ Metric: views / channel median ("outlier score"). Grade **B**.
- "Titles with 5 words have the highest median engagement"; "performance declines steadily after 6 words".
- Titles "≤30 characters receive ~60% more views"; "near 70 characters perform the worst".
- "35% of titles include numbers" and receive "~11% fewer views on average". (Contradicts BuzzSumo and Upworthy; see synthesis.)
- High-readability titles "~20% more views than the average video".
- Negative framing "~22% more views than positive titles"; only "11% of titles are classified as negative".
- "Neutral or purely informational titles consistently underperform."
- Thumbnail text: "84% of thumbnails include text" but those with text get "~19% fewer views"; best performers use "Fewer than 10 characters covering <7% of the image".
- "Dark thumbnails consistently underperform."

**OverseerOS, 7,935 million-view titles, 196 channels** - https://www.overseeros.com/blog/should-youtube-titles-be-questions Grade **B** (matched pairs within channel).
- In 891 within-channel matched pairs, question titles won 445, statements 446; "median ratio of 1.000". Only 6.8% of million-view titles used a question mark.

## 4. Headline experiments (news) - the strongest causal evidence that exists

| # | source | verbatim | grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 | Robertson et al., "Negativity drives online news consumption", Nature Human Behaviour 2023. PDF: https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/94951/1/s41562-023-01538-4.pdf | "a series of randomized controlled trials (N=22,743) ... ~105,000 different variations ... 5.7 million clicks across more than 370 million overall impressions ... For a headline of average length, each additional negative word increased the click-through rate by 2.3%." | 51 | same | Emotions: "a one standard deviation increase in sadness increases the odds ... by 0.7%, while a one standard deviation increase in joy or **fear decreases** the odds ... by 0.9% and 0.7%"; anger not significant. | 52 | same | Also: "the CTR increases as the text length increases" (headlines averaged ~15 words). | A (but news, not covers) |
| 53 | Banerjee & Urminsky, "The Language That Drives Engagement", Marketing Science 44(3), 2025. ## 6. Copywriting classics - what was tested and what is opinion

### 6.1 John Caples, *Tested Advertising Methods* (5th ed., 1997; first ed. 1932)
Library record: https://archive.org/details/testedadvertisin0000capl . Caples' method is keyed-coupon split testing (same space, same publication, different headline, count orders) - genuine A/B testing decades before the web.

| # | verbatim | grade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 62 | "I have seen one mail-order advertisement actually sell, not twice as much, not three times as much, but 19-1/2 times as much merchandise as another ad for the same product ... The difference was that one used the right appeal and the other used the wrong appeal." | A (his test, no raw data) |
| 63 | Tested pair: "ARE YOU AFRAID OF MAKING MISTAKES IN ENGLISH?" vs "DO YOU MAKE THESE MISTAKES IN ENGLISH?" - "The second headline produced far more inquiries and orders ... The word \"these\" ... arouses the readers' curiosity and self-interest." (The winner ran for 40+ years: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwin_Cody ) | **A** |
| 64 | Tested: "How you can get a loan of $200" vs "When can a loan help you?" - "brought two and a half times as many telephone calls". Account exec: "When people are in trouble, they don't want philosophy - they want money!" | **A** |
| 65 | Tested: "How to have a cool, quiet bedroom - even on hot nights" "averaged two and a half times as many calls" as "Get rid of that humidity." | **A** |
| 66 | Five rules (p.31): "1. First and foremost, try to get self-interest into every headline you write." "3. Avoid headlines that merely provoke curiosity. Curiosity combined with news or self-interest is an excellent aid to the pulling power of your headline, but curiosity by itself is seldom enough." "4. Avoid, when possible, headlines that paint the gloomy or negative side of the picture." "5. Try to suggest in your headline that here is a quick and easy way". | B/C (generalized from his tests; rule 4 is contradicted by #50 and #56 for clicks) |
| 67 | "For every curiosity headline that succeeds in getting results, a dozen will fail." / "Specifics are more believable than generalities." | C |

Note the interesting agreement between #63 and #51: the *fear* framing ("Are you afraid...") lost to the *specific mistakes* framing, and fear words lowered CTR in the Upworthy RCTs. Loss framing works; fear vocabulary does not.

| 74 | "Some headlines are 'blind.' They don't say what the product is, or what it will do for you. They are about 20 per cent below average in recall." | B |
| 75 | Tourism chapter: "People dream about visiting foreign countries. The job of your advertising is to convert their dreams into action. This can best be done by combining mouth-watering photographs with specific how-to-do-it information. You show a photograph of an ancient Oxford college, and tell the reader how much it costs to go and see it." | C (directly our format: photo + cost) |
| 76 | "For most Americans, cost is the biggest obstacle, followed, I believe, by fear." ... "Try to put your country on the map, with headlines like Suddenly everyone is going to Ruritania." | D |

### 6.3 Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966)
Library record: https://archive.org/details/bwb_S0-CRS-216 . Pure theory, no data (grade D), but the most useful *frame* for choosing a hook.

| # | verbatim | grade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 77 | "Your headline has only one job - to stop your prospect and compel him to read the second sentence of your ad." | D |
| 78 | "If he is aware of your product, and realizes that it can satisfy his desire, your headline starts with your product. If he is not aware of your product, but only of the desire itself, your headline starts with the desire. And, if he is not yet aware of what he really seeks, but is concerned only with a general problem, your headline starts with that problem and crystallizes it into a specific need." | D |

For a travel feed, the viewer is at the "desire" or "problem" stage (wants a trip / fears overpaying, fears tourist traps), never "product aware". So the cover starts with the destination + desire/problem, never with the app.

## 8. Catalogue of named hook formulas

Each entry: pattern - a real example - evidence and grade.

1. **Curiosity gap / forward reference** ("this", "these", "why", "what happened"). Example: Caples' "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?" (40+ years). Evidence: Upworthy forward-reference effect (A, prelim #54); Kuiken signal words (B+ #55); Caples split test (A #63).
2. **Specific + curiosity (the "these" move)**. Example: "These Are the Asteroids to Worry About" (Veritasium, 1.5M -> 14M). Evidence: Veritasium (C #22), Caples (A #63), Ogilvy specifics (C #73).
3. **Promise and pay-off / expectation match** (not a phrasing, a rule). Example: MrBeast's bouncy-castle colour rule. Evidence: MrBeast (C #4), Chucky 30/30 A/B on watch time (A #13), YouTube help (B #16-17).
4. **Extremity upgrade** ("Spent" -> "Survived"; opinion -> superlative). Example: "Bananas Are The Worst Food On Earth" vs "I Don't Like Bananas". Evidence: MrBeast (C #3), vidIQ specific superlative 1.5x (B).
5. **Negative superlative** ("worst", "never"). Example: "The worst ... in ..." Evidence: Outbrain +30% / +63% vs positive (B- #56), Upworthy negative words (A #50).
6. **Warning / loss avoidance** ("Don't", "Stop", "Never"). Example (vidIQ frame): "Do not ... before ..." Evidence: vidIQ nearly 2x (B); Upworthy negativity (A); but fear words reduce CTR (A #51).
7. **Mistakes**. Example: "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?"; Kat's "5 money traps you should avoid in your 20s". Evidence: Caples (A #63), Kat (C #44).
8. **Self-interest / benefit with a concrete amount**. Example: "How You Can Get a Loan of $200" (2.5x the calls). Evidence: Caples (A #64, #65), Ogilvy benefit (B #69).
9. **Cost reveal**. Examples: Kat "How much I saved in Q1 Jan-March 2025"; Ogilvy: photo + "how much it costs to go and see it". Evidence: Caples money specificity (A #64), Ogilvy tourism (C #75), Kat (C). No large study isolates "price in title".
10. **Numbers / listicle**. Example: "5 money traps you should avoid in your 20s". Evidence: mixed - Upworthy + (A prelim), BuzzSumo 3-10 (B), 1of10 -11% (B), Kuiken n.s. (B+).
11. **Secret / hidden / insider** ("Nobody knows", "Inside X", "underrated"). Example (our own census top-1): "Secret places in italy i need to see". Evidence: vidIQ "Nobody knows" >2.5x, "Inside X" 2.5x (B); Hoyos power words "secret", "banned" (C).
12. **Forbidden / banned**. Example: "McDonald's banned this item" (Hoyos). Evidence: vidIQ >2x (B), Hoyos (C #39).
13. **Us vs them / locals vs tourists**. Example (our census): "Small ways I avoided looking like a 'tourist' in Italy" (4.6M). Evidence: no controlled study; nearest is vidIQ insider frames (B). Ogilvy names the travel fear of looking foreign (D #76). Grade for the frame itself: **C/D**.
14. **Identity callout / flag word / localization** ("If you're flying to X"). Example: Ogilvy "FOR MINNEAPOLIS: 2 BELOW ZERO!". Evidence: Ogilvy (B/C #70-71), Mailchimp localization (B #59), Kallaway self-select (C).
15. **Transformation (X into Y)**. Example: "Turning X into Y". Evidence: vidIQ >3x (B).
16. **Witnessed / proof** ("caught on camera", real photo as evidence). Evidence: vidIQ >4x (B). Carousel analogue: "what X actually looks like" with real photos.
17. **Bounded journey (X days/hours)**. Example: "Photographing the Milky Way in 10 minutes, 1 hour, and 24 hours" (Galloway client, 2-3K -> 1M+). Evidence: vidIQ nearly 1.5x (B), Galloway case (C #31).
18. **Pattern interrupt / contrarian snapback**. Example: "the screen is actually the least impressive part" (8M). Evidence: Kallaway (C #40); TikTok "surprise" guidance (D #37).
19. **Person + conflict + change of mind** (slideshow). Example: "My landlord said I can't change anything so I showed her..." Evidence: self-reported (C, unverified #43); Stronger Mobile tension-then-release (C #42); Upworthy "social stories about individuals" + (A prelim).
20. **Scenario / POV**. Example: "POV: ..." Evidence: ClipSpeed 3.0x-5.9x over questions, reverses in consumer niches (B- #41).
21. **News / bandwagon**. Example: Ogilvy "Suddenly everyone is going to Ruritania". Evidence: Ogilvy news recall +22% (B #69); Caples rule 2 (B/C).
22. **Question** - included because it is common, not because it works. Evidence against: vidIQ 0.82x, Kuiken, ClipSpeed, OverseerOS tie (B). Use only for a real dispute ("Is X worth it?").
23. **Confession ("I was wrong")** - evidence against: vidIQ <0.5x (B).

## 9. What fits a Hebrew travel photo-carousel cover of 10 words or fewer

Constraints this cover lives under:
- It is title *and* thumbnail in one frame, read at scroll speed: 5-10 words per second (TikTok #34), "glance of a second or two" (#29). Aim for **5-8 words**, 10 is a ceiling.
- The photos must pay off the promise by slide 2 (MrBeast #4-5; Mosseri #47 means slide 2 can itself be shown as the cover, so slide 2 must also stand alone).
- Travel audiences save more than they comment (Orange142 #46): promise something worth keeping (a list, a cost, a route, a warning).
- Project rules for our text: never "VPN"/"proxy"/"browser"; **no price claim about what the app saves**; any number on the cover must be a real, checkable trip fact that the slides show. HotelMozil is the only brand named in a deck.

Ranked by strength of evidence x fit:

| rank | formula stack | why it fits | evidence | Hebrew example (<=10 words) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Destination + mistake/loss + forward reference** (#1+#7+#14) | Caples' 40-year winner pattern; loss beats fear; place name = identity callout | A (Caples, Upworthy) + B | "הטעויות שכל ישראלי עושה ברומא" |
| 2 | **Warning callout** ("before you fly to X") (#6+#14) | vidIQ warning frame ~2x; identity callout; open loop resolved in slides | B + A (negativity) | "טסים לאתונה? אל תזמינו לפני שתראו את זה" |
| 3 | **Insider / locals vs tourists** (#11+#13) | Top of our own English census; vidIQ insider frames 2.5x | B / C | "מה מקומיים בליסבון יודעים ותיירים לא" |
| 4 | **Negative specific superlative** (#5+#4) | Outbrain +30%; MrBeast extremity; specific, not "best" | B- / C | "המלכודת הכי יקרה לתיירים בברצלונה" |
| 5 | **Cost reveal with a real number** (#9+#2) | Ogilvy's tourism formula (photo + cost); Caples money specificity | A (Caples) / C | "כמה באמת עלה לנו שבוע באמלפי" |
| 6 | **Bounded journey** (#17) | vidIQ ~1.5x; carousel slides = days | B / C | "3 ימים בדולומיטים. את זה לא ציפינו לראות" |
| 7 | **Secret / hidden + destination** (#11) | vidIQ "nobody knows" >2.5x; works only if the place is genuinely little-known | B | "חוף ביוון שאף אחד עוד לא מכיר" |

Avoid on the cover:
- Questions as the whole hook ("?" at the end) - 0.82x and worse in three independent sets.
- Positive superlatives with no information ("הכי יפה", "מדהים") - joy/positive words lower CTR (A).
- Fear vocabulary ("מפחיד", "מסוכן") - fear words lowered CTR in the RCTs (A); use loss words ("טעות", "מלכודת", "אל").
- Confessions ("טעיתי לגבי...") - <0.5x (B).
- The word "free" ("חינם") in travel - Mailchimp found it hurts in travel specifically (B).
- Blind hooks that hide the destination - Ogilvy: blind headlines ~20% below average recall (B); Kallaway: topic clarity first.
- Any promise the photos cannot show by slide 2.

Open items (to test, not to assume):
- Every Hebrew example above is a D until it is run against our own data (`census-tables.md` Hebrew sets, `04-hebrew-voice.md` top-vs-bottom third).
- Numbers on covers: evidence is split; worth an A/B on our own posts (same photos, cover with vs without the number).
